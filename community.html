The Commentators of the Catena Aurea

St. Thomas Aquinas wove together the words of over eighty Church Fathers and ecclesiastical writers. Here are some of the most frequently cited:

St. John Chrysostom

c. 349 – 407 AD

Archbishop of Constantinople and Doctor of the Church. Known as "Golden-Mouth" (Chrysostomos) for his extraordinary eloquence. His homilies on Matthew's Gospel are among the most frequently quoted sources in the Catena Aurea. He emphasized the practical, moral application of Scripture to daily life.

St. Augustine of Hippo

354 – 430 AD

Bishop of Hippo in North Africa and Doctor of the Church. One of the most influential theologians in Western Christianity. His works on grace, free will, and the Trinity shaped Catholic theology for centuries. His commentaries in the Catena tend toward deep theological and allegorical interpretation.

St. Jerome

c. 342 – 420 AD

Priest, theologian, and Doctor of the Church. Best known for translating the Bible into Latin (the Vulgate). His commentary on Matthew is one of the most cited in the Catena Aurea. Jerome brought extraordinary knowledge of Hebrew, Greek, and the Holy Land to his biblical interpretation.

St. Hilary of Poitiers

c. 310 – 367 AD

Bishop of Poitiers and Doctor of the Church, sometimes called the "Athanasius of the West" for his defense of Trinitarian orthodoxy against Arianism. His commentary on Matthew is the earliest surviving Latin commentary on any Gospel.

Origen of Alexandria

c. 185 – 253 AD

Early Church scholar and one of the most prolific writers of early Christianity. While some of his speculative theology was later condemned, his biblical commentaries remain deeply influential. He favored allegorical interpretation and is heavily cited in the Catena Aurea.

St. Gregory the Great

c. 540 – 604 AD

Pope and Doctor of the Church. Known for his pastoral sensitivity and clear teaching. His homilies on the Gospels combine theological depth with practical guidance for Christian living.

The Venerable Bede

c. 672 – 735 AD

English Benedictine monk, scholar, and Doctor of the Church. His Gospel commentaries draw extensively on earlier Fathers and are valued for their clarity and careful synthesis.

Remigius of Auxerre

c. 841 – 908 AD

Benedictine monk and teacher. His Gospel commentaries, frequently cited in the Catena Aurea, served as important educational texts in the Carolingian period.
