Questions & Discussion

Difficult passages, open questions, and the Fathers' wisdom

✎

Reading the Gospels with the Church Fathers often raises as many questions as it answers! This page is a place to bring those questions—confusing passages, difficult teachings, or simply things you'd like to discuss further as a group.

Each question below includes the passage reference and, where helpful, the relevant commentary from the Catena Aurea itself. You can find the full text online at eCatholic2000 to look up and copy the exact quote.

Have a question? Send it to Amanda or Fran with the passage reference (e.g., "Matthew 5:22") and they will post it here. If you found a helpful quote from the Catena Aurea commentary, include that too!
✦

Current Questions

Matthew 5:22 — On Anger

What does Our Lord mean by "without a cause" in "whosoever is angry with his brother without a cause shall be in danger of the judgment"? Does all anger constitute sin, or only unjust anger?

St. Augustine: "Many Latin manuscripts have the words 'without cause,' which is not found in the Greek copies. But assuming the reading 'without cause,' he is angry without cause who is angry where he has no right to be angry. That anger which has just cause is not what is here forbidden."

Discussed at our meeting on [date]. To be continued.

Matthew 3:11 — Baptism with Fire

What is the "baptism with the Holy Ghost and with fire" that John the Baptist speaks of? Is this the same as Pentecost, or something else?

St. John Chrysostom: "By fire here He signifies the abundance of grace; for He says 'fire,' showing the warm and unquenchable nature of grace."

Open question for discussion.

Matthew 1:18–25 — St. Joseph's Doubt

Why did St. Joseph plan to "put her away privily"? Was he doubting Mary, or was he acting out of humility?

St. Jerome: "This is a testimony to Mary's character, that Joseph, knowing her chastity and wondering at what had happened, covered in silence that which he could not understand."

Open question for discussion.

How to Look Up the Commentary

When you encounter a confusing passage, here's how to find what the Church Fathers said about it:

  1. Go to the Catena Aurea online
  2. Navigate to the Gospel and chapter you're reading
  3. Find the verse numbers that contain the passage in question
  4. Read through the Fathers' commentaries for that section
  5. Copy the relevant quote and include it when you submit your question

When sharing a quote, please include: who said it (which Church Father), what passage they're commenting on, and the quote itself.
